ASTEROIDS II for the VZ300 - a sequel to VZ Asteroids by Dubois & McNamara

Blast, split and shatter your way through escalating waves in this all-new arcade sequel to the classic
VZ Asteroids by Dubois & McNamara.
- Crack ore rocks for spinning jewels and chain them for up to x4 points.
- Set off volatile rocks for chain-reaction shrapnel.
- Duel raider saucers, aiming hunters, diving stingers and creeping mines.
- Grab spread shot, rapid fire, shields and smart bombs.

TECH SPECS
- Needs a VZ300 with the BennVenn SD loader / 128K. It loads as a single 23.5 KB .VZ from SD.
  100% Z80 machine code.
- Display:
  - 128x64 four-colour graphics in deep-blue space, at 25 fps.
  - Every frame is built off-screen and copied to the screen during the vertical blank, so there's no
    flicker and no tearing.
  - Objects wrap smoothly around all four edges.
- Graphics:
  - 155 sprites, including a 32-direction ship and 80 frames of tumbling rocks.
  - All pre-shifted at start-up into a 14.5 KB cache, using the BennVenn's extra SRAM bank.
- Effects: a 40-particle system (exhaust, debris, sparks, sparkles), shock-wave rings, screen flash and
  shake, and a three-layer parallax starfield.
- Gameplay:
  - Smooth fixed-point physics.
  - Four rock types in three sizes, each with its own splitting rules.
  - Four enemy types with their own tactics.
  - Jewel chains, five power-ups, an extra ship every 10,000 points, and a high-score table.
- Sound:
  - A software three-voice engine on the VZ's single 1-bit speaker: title music, plus up to three
    sound effects at once in game, including the classic heartbeat.
  - It plays only in the CPU's spare time, so it never slows the game.
- Attract mode: animated title, Enemy Files and Treasure screens, a self-playing demo, and high scores.
- Controls: keyboard, or a VZ joystick (detected automatically).

WHY THE BENNVENN?
Asteroids II uses about 42 KB of memory while it runs:
- program 18 KB
- screen buffer and game tables 7 KB
- pre-shifted sprite cache 14.5 KB
- variables, particles and stack 2 KB
(The 3 KB of graphics source data is used once at start-up and then overwritten.)

A stock VZ300 has 16 KB of user RAM, and a standard 16K expansion only takes that to 32 KB. The
BennVenn's 128K adds an extra 10 KB SRAM bank at $4000. That bank holds most of the sprite cache, which
is what lets every rock tumble smoothly through 8 frames with no time spent shifting graphics while you
play. The SD loader also loads the whole 23.5 KB game in seconds.

The original VZ Asteroids needed an expanded machine too (a 16K RAM pack). A cut-down version for a
VZ300 with a standard 16K expansion would be possible, with fewer animation frames.
