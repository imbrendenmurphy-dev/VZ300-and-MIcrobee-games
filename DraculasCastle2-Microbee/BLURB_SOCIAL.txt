🦇 DRACULA'S CASTLE II: THE CASTLE AWAKENS 🦇
for the Microbee Premium Plus
"Something ancient has woken."
VIBED BY BPM 2026

Transylvania, 1897. The villagers of Borgo Pass whisper of strange lights in the old castle on the hill. Folk have vanished. Graves lie open. The dead walk.

📜 FROM THE VZ300
Back in the 1980s, Dracula's Castle was a BASIC maze game for the VZ, and you needed the 16K expansion just to play it. I bought it, and the castle I imagined was far grander than the simple maze on screen. DRACULA'S CASTLE II is that imagined game, finally built: a first-person gothic RPG for the VZ300 (BennVenn SD card + 128K) in the spirit of Bard's Tale and Dungeon Master. It has six castle areas, a separate animated battle screen and a hidden tribute to the 1984 original.

Now the castle wakes up on the Microbee Premium Plus. The game is the VZ's own: the same maps, monsters, stats, story and music. Every picture is redrawn in the Microbee's 16-colour character graphics, and the display and combat are rebuilt for speed.

Walk the castle in first person through six areas: the Grounds, the Great Hall, the Library, the Dungeons, the Clock Tower and Dracula's Tower. Talk to the ghosts who still haunt it, buy potions from a prisoner through the bars, find the keys that open each new wing, and ring the great bell that wakes the Count.

Fight twelve "groovy ghoulie" monsters in quick turn-based battles. The bat, skeleton, ghost, zombie, slime, knight and bride fight in whichever room you meet them, while the Gargoyle, Grell, Ghoul King, Bat Lord and Dracula wait in their own lairs. Swing your sword, cast fire, throw holy water, and watch them dissolve.

🕯️ FEATURES
▪ Six areas in first person, each with its own walls, floors, decorations and furniture. Turning takes about a tenth of a second.
▪ All twelve monsters redrawn as cartoon "groovy ghoulies", with idle, attack and hit poses. The ghost floats through eight poses.
▪ Battles in the room you're in: a skeleton in the library fights among the bookshelves.
▪ Drawn battle effects: a sword arc, a fireball that bursts into embers, holy water and a gold cross, claw rakes, level-up sparkles, and an ordered dissolve when a monster dies.
▪ Fast, aggressive combat. A one-hit skeleton fight takes about three seconds from encounter to back in the maze.
▪ An illustrated automap with a compass and legend, plus an inventory and status page and gold-ruled story pages.
▪ Monochrome mode for amber and green monitors, with its own title and artwork.
▪ The VZ's three-voice beeper music, with a SOFT voicing made for the Microbee's speaker, or the original VZ sound.
▪ An attract sequence: the title, the story so far, a tour of the six areas and the Ghoul Gallery of all twelve monsters, each slide opening with a soft chime.
▪ The four hidden 1984 tribute screens from the VZ game.

💾 TECH SPECS
▪ Machine: Microbee Premium Plus. Z80 at 3.375 MHz, 1MB RAM, 32K PCG RAM, 6545 CRTC.
▪ Display: 40 × 32 character cells of 8 × 8 pixels (320 × 256), 16 colours, a colour pair per cell. Separate artwork for one-colour monitors.
▪ First-person view: a 26 × 24-cell window composed from wall, decoration and object pieces. Each area's art (up to about 1,700 glyphs) stays unpacked in RAM, and floor objects are merged into a spare PCG bank as they come into view. A turn redraws in 4 to 5 frames.
▪ Monsters: drawn cell-exact, so every cell a monster touches holds only the monster and black, and lays cleanly over any room. Rooms use PCG banks 0 to 4, monsters banks 5 to 13, the font banks 14 and 15.
▪ Combat: each animation step redraws only the cells that change, just after the frame sync, staying ahead of the beam. Effects are pixel-merged into a spare PCG bank.
▪ Map and story pages stay unpacked after their first use. The map opens in under half a second, and its "you are here" marker blinks without stalling the music.
▪ Loading: a buffered decompressor. Entering an area also unpacks its room and its two or three likely monsters, so most fights load in about a third of a second.
▪ Sound: the VZ game's three-voice pulse beeper engine, retimed for the Premium (210 T-state loop, about 16 kHz), with a frame-locked note sequencer.
▪ Disk: DS80 (80 tracks × 2 sides × 10 × 512 bytes). Boots to CP/M; DRACULA.COM loads 21 files (about 660K) and the game then takes over the machine.
▪ Saving: RAM checkpoints at the stone angels, as on the VZ. They are lost at power-off.
▪ Source: about 11,800 lines of Z80 assembly, plus about 6,000 lines of Python tools that draw the art, pack the areas and monsters, check every screen against reference pictures and build the disk.

🧠 RAM USED
The game runs from the upper 32K and uses 31 of the Premium Plus's 32 blocks of 32K:
▪ Block 1: the VZ game's working RAM (maps, text grid, panels)
▪ Block 2: unpacking scratch
▪ Block 3: the combat screen (composed frames, room backdrop, masks)
▪ Blocks 4–13 and 15–23: packed art, maps, text and music (19 banks, about 600K)
▪ Block 14: character shapes for the text screens
▪ Block 24: the map page, unpacked
▪ Block 25: the story page, unpacked
▪ Blocks 26, 27 and 29: the current area's battle room and its likely monsters, unpacked
▪ Block 28: the program image, then the current area's maze art
▪ Blocks 30 and 31: the ghost's poses and the area's maze pieces

🎮 TO PLAY
Boot DRACULA.DS8, type DRACULA, then press C for colour or M for monochrome.
▪ W / I / up: step forward
▪ A / J / left and D / L / right: turn
▪ S / K / down: turn around
▪ SPACE / RETURN: act, talk, open, select
▪ M map · E inventory · P pause (Q quits)
▪ N music on/off · - SOFT music · + VZ music
▪ Joystick on PIO port A
