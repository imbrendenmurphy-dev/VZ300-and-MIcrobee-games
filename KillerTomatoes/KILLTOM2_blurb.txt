ATTACK OF THE KILLER TOMATOES - RE-SAUCED!
for the Dick Smith VZ300 / Laser 310
ViBED by BPM 2026 - after the 1984 original by Apolon Ivankovic

San Diego. A quiet summer evening. Suddenly... the salad bowl strikes back!

Killer tomatoes are rolling through town, the army is baffled, and the scientists
are mostly hiding. Only Mason Dixon stands in their way, armed with a mallet and
a box of homemade traps.

The 1984 VZ classic's trap-and-whack idea is back, rebuilt from scratch in Z80
assembly. Build a trap, wait for a tomato to blunder in, then whack it until it
goes SPLAT. Do it before it struggles free, and before its friends catch up
with you.

Fight through three worlds, each with its own look and hazards:
  1. San Diego Suburbs - BBQ grills and manholes
  2. The Cannery - conveyor belts and steam vents
  3. Top Secret Lab - zappers and teleports
Each world ends in a boss fight: Mama Beefsteak, Baron von Beefsteak, and
finally The Great Tomato, which only one terrible love song can hurt.

FEATURES
- Mode 1 title screen with a giant animated tomato that blinks, glares and
  chomps, dripping title lettering, twinkling stars and the odd lightning flash
- Full attract sequence: typed story, cast pages, "WANTED!" boss posters,
  power-up guide, self-playing demo and the Hall of Sauce high scores
- 14 stages: 9 mazes, 3 bosses and 2 "Tomato Smash" bonus rounds, then an
  ending and a harder Second Helping lap
- 8 tomato types, each with its own personality:
    Cherry Tom, Roma Runner (chases you), Big Beefsteak (splits in two),
    Unripe Greenie (traps don't work), Golden Tomato (rare, flees),
    Jumping Jack (dodges traps), Rotten Ronnie (leaves a stink),
    Cannonball (rolls in straight lines)
- Power-ups:
    Ketchup Gun (3 upgrade levels), Sneakers, Sauce Boss (double points),
    Colander helmet (shield), Disguise, Soup Can (extra life), and
    PUBERTY LOVE, the record that sends every tomato running
- Collect the S-A-U-C-E letters for a bonus and an extra life
- Combo scoring, a golden-tomato chase, news flashes ("SALSA DECLARED
  ILLEGAL"), splat particles, BONK/SNAP pop-ups, hit and screen flashes
- Smooth, flicker-free sprites drawn racing the TV beam, at 50 frames a second
- Beeper music and sound effects that play in each frame's spare time, so the
  game never slows down

QUALITY OF LIFE
- Play on the keyboard or on either joystick: FIRE builds traps and whacks,
  ARM fires ketchup
- Music on/off at any time (S)
- Pause and quit to title
- Easy / Normal / Hard
- Trainer mode with infinite lives (no high score recorded)
- Level select, including the bosses
- Continue from the stage you were on
- Pick traps back up
- Short invulnerability after respawning, and fair, forgiving hit boxes
- Type your initials straight in for the high-score table

TECHNICAL SPECS
  Machine ........ Dick Smith VZ300 / Laser 310 (Z80 @ 3.54 MHz, PAL 50 Hz)
  Memory ......... BennVenn 128K SD expansion, or the standard 16K RAM pack
                   (RAM to $F7FF needed; a stock 16K VZ300 is not enough)
  File ........... KILLTOM2.VZ - machine code (type F1), 22,355 bytes
  Load address ... $8000-$D752, runs automatically on load
  Graphics ....... Mode 1, 128 x 64, 4 colours, both colour sets used
                   (each world has its own palette)
  Playfield ...... 16 x 11 cells of 8 x 5 pixels, plus a status bar
  Sprites ........ 8 x 5 masked sprites at 2-pixel steps, bosses up to 24 x 15
  Sound .......... 1-bit beeper: pulse-width envelope music in game,
                   two-voice melody and bass on the front-end screens,
                   9 tunes, 19 sound effects
  Code ........... 100% Z80 assembly; no ROM calls, interrupts off
  Hardware ....... no mapper writes, DOS area at $FA00+ left untouched
  Controls ....... keyboard: Q A O P (or M ,) to move, SPACE = trap/whack,
                   Z = ketchup, RETURN = pause, S = music
                   joystick 1 or 2: stick, FIRE, ARM
  Title options .. D = skill, T = trainer, L = level select

Original game: "Attack of the Killer Tomatoes" for the VZ200/300 by Apolon
Ivankovic, January 1984. Inspired by the 1978 film. All music is original.
