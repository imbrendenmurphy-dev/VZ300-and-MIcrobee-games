TIGER GATE — a Karateka-style martial arts adventure for the Dick Smith VZ300
VIBED BY BPM 2026

Kurokage's raiders have burned the dojo at Three Pines and dragged old Master Hoshi up the mountain to the fortress beyond the Tiger Gate. Ren, his last student, ties on his master's red sash and follows: alone and barehanded.

This video is a full AI-vs-AI playthrough, captured straight from the cycle-accurate emulator with real beeper sound: the title screen and opening story, then all six duels back to back, through to the ending and credits.

TIGER GATE is built in the spirit of Karateka. You don't win by mashing buttons — you win by reading your opponent: watch the wind-up, keep your distance, and strike as he commits. Six stages climb the mountain road, each with its own scenery, entrance, opponent and way of fighting:

1. The Rope Bridge — a waterfall thunders below as the straw-hatted Bridge Keepers bar the way.
2. The Outer Court — cherry blossom drifts past the stone lanterns as horned-helmet guards come at you in turn.
3. The Lantern Hall — the Lantern Monk kneels in silence until you come close. He guards and counters.
4. The Cliff Path — boulders crash down wherever a green shadow falls, then the Cliff Scout attacks with a flying kick.
5. The Inner Gate — torches and banners under a red moon, ending with the Gate Oni's slow, crushing blows.
6. The Tiger Throne — at sunset on the summit, Kurokage himself. He feints, he flurries, and he gets faster as he weakens.

FEATURES
- Layered parallax scenery in all four mode 1 colours: sky, sun and mountains scroll at a quarter speed behind full-speed walls, railings and pillars, over a perspective floor that converges and slides as you run.
- Animated details: a waterfall, flickering lanterns, torches, waving banners, drifting petals, leaves and embers.
- Expressive fighters with Karateka-style stances, wind-ups, punches and kicks at three heights, guards, bows, recoil, knock-downs and falls — every opponent has a distinct silhouette.
- Opponents that think differently: spacing, evasion, guarding, counter-punching, hit-and-run, heavy telegraphed blows, feints and changes of rhythm.
- Clean hits feel heavy: hit pauses, knock-back, a full-screen flash on counters and knockouts, and gongs, swishes, blocks, thuds and footsteps on the one-bit beeper.
- Three-voice title and ending themes (bass, melody and harmony mixed on the one-bit speaker), with sparse, suspenseful single-note music during play.
- The full release: custom logo and font, illustrated title screen, a four-picture opening story, stage cards, story scenes at the turning points, results screens, a proper ending with a cast roll and credits, an attract-mode demo duel and the Hall of Masters high-score table.
- Forgiving by design: a 12-pip health bar that slowly recovers while you stay unhit, instant retry of the current duel, every stage a checkpoint, and a start-from-stage option. Easy, Normal and Hard difficulty; music and effects toggle separately.
- Run back to the left between bouts to take in the scenery.

TECH SPECS
Machine: Dick Smith VZ300 (Z80A, 3.5469 MHz)
Expansion: BennVenn SD Loader with 128 KB SRAM
Display: Mode 1, 128 x 64 pixels, 4 colours, colour composite output
Frame rate: 25 game ticks a second, locked to field sync, no interrupts
Rendering: fight band composited off-screen and copied in the vertical blank; top layers redrawn only when they change
Parallax: far layer at 1/4 speed, mid layer at full speed, blue-keyed through to the far layer by lookup tables
Floor: 4 precomputed perspective phases per stage
Fighters: 32 x 24 px masked sprites, 21 poses each — Ren plus 6 opponent types, 147 frames in all
Sound: one-bit speaker; a 3-voice direct-digital-synthesis player (~16.6 kHz) for the themes, pulse-timed effects and noise for combat
Loading: each stage's scenery, opponents, AI, script and music load from SD between stages, in under a second
Data files: header, number and XOR checksum on every file; a missing or damaged file shows its name, with retry or back to title
Code: about 1,700 lines of Z80 assembly; art, music and data generated by Python tools

RAM USED
Of the 36 KB from $7000 to $FFFF, only 2.4 KB ($BE80-$C7FF) is spare — the rest is video RAM, game variables, the resident engine/sound/fighter frames (15.6 KB), the current stage's loaded pack (8-10 KB), the composited render buffer, the DOS stack during loads, and the SD sector buffer, with about 1.5 KB left over for the BennVenn's own DOS. The game also uses 8 KB of the BennVenn's SRAM for the stage's opponent frames — about 44 KB of RAM in use altogether.
