GHOST HUNTER 3-D  -  THE BIG HAUNT
for the Dick Smith VZ300 (BennVenn SD loader + 128K RAM)
ViBED by BPM 2026

The ghosts have moved out of the little one-screen maze and into the
big house. Ghost Hunter 3-D takes everything from Ghost Hunter 2 into
full arcade-sized mazes several screens big:

  - the four ghost personalities
  - power-ups, combos and streaks
  - bonus rounds, intermissions and the full attract show

The VZ's screen glides smoothly around each maze as you hunt. Ghosts
lurking off-screen give themselves away as coloured blips at the edge of
the view. The walls are raised, bas-relief slabs with real depth. When
everything goes quiet, eyes peep out of the cracks to watch you go by,
and candles flicker on the stonework.

Six haunted mazes - The Crypt, Haunted Halls, The Graveyard,
The Catacombs, The Belfry and Misty Manor - plus the Ghost Buffet and
Pellet Panic bonus rounds and two cartoon intermissions.

Happy haunting!

TECH SPECS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Machine      Dick Smith VZ300 (VZ200 compatible display), Z80 @ 3.54 MHz
Needs        BennVenn SD loader + 128K RAM expansion (SRAM banks)
File         GHOST3.VZ, 26,176 bytes, loads low at $8000-$E63F
Display      Mode 1, 128x64, 4 colours (CSS0, CSS1 flashes)
Frame rate   50 fps, no interrupts, synced to the 6847 field sync
Controls     Q/A/M/, keys or the VZ joystick; P pause, S sound
             Fire or Space starts a game

Mazes        6 main mazes, 27 cells wide x 31-35 cells tall
             (162 x 155-175 pixels = about 3.5 screens each),
             404-464 pellets and 18-20 power pills each (about 1 per
             24 pellets, the same ratio as Ghost Hunter 2), teleporters
             and tunnels. Pellet Panic bonus maze 27x15 (8 pills).
             Every maze is machine-checked: fully connected,
             no dead ends, no 2x2 open areas.
Cells        6x5 pixels; actors move in pixels, speeds in 8.8 fixed
             point with 2:3 vertical scaling (VZ pixel aspect)
Viewport     128 x 58 pixels with a 6-row HUD; camera dead zone;
             1-pixel steps both ways; teleports and respawns
             "swoop" the camera across the maze
Scrolling    The maze lives in SRAM bank A ($4000, port $37=1) as a
             ring of 58 pixel rows x 168 bytes:
               - each row is stored 4 times, pre-shifted by 0-3
                 pixels, so any camera position is a byte copy
               - map row R lives in ring slot R mod 58
               - a camera step renders only the one new row, from
                 tiles, into all 4 phases
Rendering    Camera moved: the viewport is redrawn by generated,
             unrolled stack-copy code (POP/PUSH, about 12.8 T-states a
             byte). It goes in two halves, each followed by the
             sprites in that half, so it stays ahead of the beam
             (no tearing, no flicker).
             Camera still: GH2-style erase/redraw of the sprites
             only, restoring from the ring.
             Map edits (pellets, pulsing pills, swirling
             teleporters, wall decals) re-render just the bytes
             involved and queue a dirty rectangle.
Walls        "3-D" slabs lit from the top-left, with front faces
             where a corridor runs below. 4 styles (stone and brick,
             haunted wood, hedges, skull ossuary) of 54-59 tiles
             each, built on the VZ from each cell's 8 neighbours
             (37 shapes x row parity).
Decals       15 per maze: eyes that open, look towards the hero and
             close; candles that flicker. They animate only while
             the view is still.
Memory       Main RAM: game code + data; at run time the sprite
             shift tables, generated copy code, grid and tile pages.
             SRAM bank A: the 4-phase maze ring (9,744 bytes).
             SRAM bank B ($4000, port $37=3): the attract world
             (title, intermissions, hi-score entry, big sprites,
             title music, full-screen background), copied there at
             boot.
             Port $37 is only ever written with 1 or 3.
Sound        1-bit speaker, GH2 engine: SFX, siren and jingles mixed
             into each frame's spare time; a new title tune
             (creeping minor-key march with a walking bass)
Extras       Intermissions with sparse geometric backdrops drifting
             diagonally behind the cartoon, repainted every frame
             between the sprite erase and draw
Source       About 8,800 lines of Z80 (sjasmplus) + Python tools for
             the mazes, tiles, sprites, title art and music.
             Includes a VZ300 test emulator modelling the BennVenn
             banks, field timing and VRAM writes during the display.

Original Ghost Hunter by Dubois & McNamara.
