KABOOMERANG

Wally the demolition wombat takes his gumnut bombs and his lucky boomerang across the outback to stop Baron Von Croak, the cane toad tycoon paving the bush into car parks. Below is World 1, running on a browser model of the Premium Plus screen: 40×25 cells of 8×8 programmable characters, two colours per cell, with every moving thing composited into its own characters pixel by pixel, the way the Z80 will do it.

Animation bench

Each figure is a 3×3 cell patch of the same screen model, cycling one animation. All of these also play in the stage above: stand still to see Wally blink and look around, drop a bomb to see him crouch, stand near a blast to make his hat jump.

What the mockup proves

Square-ish chunky pixels

The 40-column mode halves the dot clock, so each PCG pixel is two dots wide. With 8-line characters that gives 320×200 with much squarer pixels, far nicer for round bombs and chunky characters than tall 8×16 or 8×11 cells.

Many colours per tile

Each 16×16 gameplay tile is four PCG cells with their own colour pairs. The sandstone, termite mounds and gumnuts use two to four pairs per tile, with dithering for shades like the orange of the mounds.

Smooth pixel movement

Characters move a pixel at a time. Each frame the sprite is ORed into private PCG cells, floor detail is dropped under it, and each cell's colour follows the sprite's colour band. Turn on Sprite cells to watch it happen.

Why this genre fits the Bee

Grid movement is axis-locked. A sprite walking sideways is always cell-aligned vertically, so it covers at most 3×2 cells and its colour bands line up with cell rows. A free-moving sprite would need 3×3 cells and clash constantly.

Controls that forgive

Corners slide you into the gap, the newest direction you press wins, a direction tapped just before a junction is remembered for a moment, and you can always walk off a bomb you just dropped.

Danger is always readable

Bombs flash red in their last second, and the Blast preview (an Easy-mode option) tints every floor tile a bomb is about to hit. Flames fill whole tiles so the danger area is never ambiguous, even mid-chain.

The game

Classic arena bombing at the core: open the maze, chain your blasts, collect power-ups, outwit the wildlife and reach the hollow-log exit. Around that sit four ideas that make it its own game.

The Kaboomerang

Wally's boomerang flies four tiles and comes back. It stuns enemies, fetches power-ups from across the arena, and if it passes a lit gumnut it snatches the bomb and drops it at the far end of its flight. Upgrades add range and a curved throw that turns one corner. Try it in the mockup: drop a bomb, step back, throw.

Burrows

Wombats dig. Standing in a burrow hole lets blasts pass over you, with only Wally's hat poking out. Burrows collapse after a few uses, so they reward planning without letting you hide forever. Later Wally can dig new ones in soft ground.

More than "clear and exit"

Stages mix objectives: herd lost joeys back to their mum's pouch, bomb pressure plates to open sluice gates, demolish the Baron's machinery against the clock, collect five golden gumnuts, or survive a stampede. Each world adds one stage mechanic and builds puzzles from it.

Hearts, continues, saves

Three hearts (up to five), flashing safety after a hit, power-ups kept when you lose a heart, a checkpoint at every stage, unlimited continues, and progress saved to disk after every stage. Easy, Normal and Hard change enemy speed, fuse length and the blast preview.

The cast

Hero

Wally Wombat

Retired demolition expert in a hard hat with headlamp. Blinks, looks around, taps his foot, crouches to set bombs, flinches at blasts, does a dust-cloud dance after every stage.

    Water channels with floating logs that carry bombs downstream, crocs, sluice gates opened by pressure plates.

  4. 4

    Ice tiles where bombs and Wally slide until they hit something, emus, snow drifts that refill.
    Boss: the Yowie, hurling snowballs that freeze bombs solid until a blast thaws them.
  5. 5
    Opal Mines
    Dark tunnels lit by Wally's headlamp (colour RAM lighting around him), mine carts on rails that ferry bombs, opal seams, bush rats.
    Boss: the Mega Mole, a drilling machine that tunnels under the arena and surfaces where the ground cracks.
  6. 6
    Croak Industries
    Conveyor floors, crushers, switch-operated doors, teleport chutes and vats that spawn toads until you blow them up.
    Boss: Baron Von Croak in the Toad-Mech, three phases, ending with the Baron fleeing in a bathtub.

Each world has seven stages and a boss stage. Six secret bush-tucker bonus stages hide behind blasted walls or opal totals, so the full count is 54.

More modes, after the campaign is solid

Battle

Two players plus up to two computer wombats in eight battle arenas, with hazards on or off, starting power-ups, win counts and a sudden-death squeeze where the walls close in.

Co-op

Wally and Pip play the campaign together. A downed partner becomes a floating hard hat that the other player touches to revive. Shared opals, separate hearts.

Survival

Endless waves on a shrinking arena, a power-up drop every wave, hi-score table saved to disk.

Demolition puzzles

Thirty single-screen puzzles with a fixed number of bombs: knock down every target in one chain.

Sound and presentation

Sound engine

Speaker on PIO port B bit 6. Two tone voices are time-sliced into one bit stream, with noise-burst drums and arpeggiated chords. On the title, interludes and fanfares the CPU is free, so music uses the full engine. In play the tune drops to slow, short blipped notes between frames so it never slows the game. Music and effects switch off separately.

Sound list

Title theme, six world themes, boss theme, bonus-stage tune, victory and world-clear fanfares, ending theme. Effects: bomb place, fuse fizz in the last second, explosions that get deeper as a chain grows, crumble, power-up sparkles, boomerang whoosh, each enemy's call, hurt, warning klaxon.

Title and attract

Large KABOOMERANG lettering built from about 300 unique PCG characters, Wally lighting the fuse on the letters, then an attract loop that replays recorded play of real stages, a hi-score table and a demo of the boomerang.

Interludes and ending

Illustrated scenes between worlds: the Baron's airship, Wally's ute driving the map, the next boss looming. Static artwork with selected parts animated. Credits close with "ViBED by BPM".

Hardware plan

Native Z80 at 3.375 MHz throughout. The engine starts from the one already proven in MICROBEE AUSTRALIAN OPEN TENNIS and HULLBREACH: page flipping, a separate sprite-glyph bank per page, restore lists and pre-shifted frames. "Proven" means shown on your own Premium Plus by one of those games.

Everything fits in RAM together with room to spare. The game loads the first world and the shared data at boot, then pulls in each further world in the background of its interlude, so disk access never happens during play except for saving.

Frame budget and worst case. The Z80 has about 67,500 T-states per 50 Hz frame. HULLBREACH, on the same engine, holds 25 fps exploring and about 22 fps in its busiest fight. Compositing one sprite cell (fetch background, mask, OR the pre-shifted frame, write 8 bytes plus colour and attribute) costs roughly 650–750 T-states, so a 6-cell sprite is about 4,200. Flames are cheap: their shapes stay resident and their flicker is mostly colour RAM changes, a few bytes per tile. The worst case of two players, eight enemies, the boomerang, six particles and a big chain comes to roughly 60,000 to 75,000 T-states. The plan is a steady 25 Hz update with 2-pixel steps, kept smooth by double-buffered sprite cells, with 50 Hz used whenever the frame allows. The readout under the stage applies this model to what is on screen; the real numbers come from the benchmark build.

Build plan

  1. Hardware test disk. 40-column mode attempts, PCG banks and attributes, palette chart, port 50h banking across all 32 banks, keyboard and joystick, vertical blank timing, disk read and write from the DOS prompt. You run it in colour and on the amber monitor and we lock the decisions.Next
  2. Graphics proof. This mockup settles the look and techniques; the Z80 version renders the same arena with composited sprites.Design done
  3. Core gameplay. Movement with buffering, bombs, chains, crumbling, power-ups, the boomerang, burrows and three enemies, playable on the machine.
  4. Benchmark. Worst-case stage with everything running plus music, measured with a cycle counter. The update rate gets fixed here.
  5. World 1 complete. Seven stages, Big Daddy Bufo, music, title screen. You play it and tune difficulty.
  6. Worlds 2–6. One world at a time, each with its mechanic, enemies, boss and interlude.
  7. Modes and presentation. Battle, co-op, survival and puzzles, attract mode, ending, saves, polish.
