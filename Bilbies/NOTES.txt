BILBIES!  for the VZ300 (BennVenn SD loader + 128K RAM expansion)
=================================================================
An original puzzle game in the "guide the little critters home" genre:
the same puzzle mechanics as ZX Spectrum Lemmings, with original
characters (bilbies), original levels, original artwork and lettering.
The title tune is an arrangement of Grieg's "In the Hall of the Mountain
King" (1875, public domain); the jingles are original.

FILES
  BILBIES.VZ              the whole game in one file (23K, loads at $8000).
                          No DAT files.
  src\                    source, build tools, level designs
  screenshots\            pictures from the emulator

LOADING
  Copy BILBIES.VZ to the SD card and run it from the BennVenn SD loader.
  It loads low ($8000), never runs from video RAM and unpacks from main RAM:
  the level data is copied into bank B first, then the program is unpacked
  in place over it.
  It needs BOTH 10K SRAM banks of the BennVenn at $4000-$67FF:
    bank A (mapping 1, as the DOS leaves it) holds the level picture,
    bank B (mapping 3) holds the 24 level designs, packed.
  If either isn't there you'll see a message saying which, in mode(0).
  Press RESET to get back to the loader.

CONTROLS
  Q A O P / joystick   move the cursor (it speeds up as you hold it).
                       Push against any edge of the playfield to scroll:
                       the levels are 512 x 80 pixels, four screens
                       across and nearly two down.
  SPACE / M / FIRE     give the selected skill to the bilby under the cursor,
                       or press a button when the cursor is on the panel.
                       Fire on the minimap jumps the view there.
  ARM / X  /  Z        next / previous skill
  1 - 8                climber, floater, bomber, blocker, builder, basher,
                       miner, digger
  ,  .                 release rate down / up (or hold fire on - / +)
  H                    pause            N  N   nuke (press twice)
  CTRL+Q               give up (back to the level menu)

  Title: FIRE / SPACE / RETURN play,  T trainer (every level open),
         K music on/off,  S sound effects on/off.
         Leave it ~30 seconds for the attract mode (five levels played
         by the computer); any key returns.
  Level menu: O P level,  Q A rating,  FIRE play,  Q back.

THE SCREEN (mode 1, 128x64, 4 colours)
  * Playfield 128 x 44 pixels, a window on a level of 512 x 80 that
    scrolls four ways.
  * Row 44: a bar showing where the window is across the level.
  * Status line (3x5 font): what's under the cursor and how many, OUT,
    IN %, and the time left.
  * Twelve buttons with counts and icons (rate - +, the 8 skills, pause,
    nuke), the selected one framed in yellow, and a minimap of the whole
    level (one dot for 16 x 8 pixels) with the bilbies on it and the window
    marked in green. Fire on the minimap jumps the window there.
  * Behind the bilbies: faint sky, star, mist and cave textures, with
    scenery that never gets in the way (gum trees, scrub, far hills,
    clouds, cave columns, crystals, ruined pillars, reeds), plus 50
    hand-drawn Australiana pictures placed through the levels.
    Set pieces:
      Burrow Down       grain silos, windpump and tank, the dunny
      Up the Bank       a station homestead with its verandah, the farm ute
      Gum Tree Gully    a shearing shed, a great granite wave rock
      Wait Here         a two-storey country pub with iron lace verandahs
      Sloping Off       a goldfields poppet head over the mine
      Moonlit Ruins     a ruined bush church, a homestead chimney
      The Jetty         a harbour beach: sand, Norfolk pines, the flags, a
                        lifesavers' tower, umbrellas, surfboards; across the
                        water the opera house sails, a big steel arch bridge
                        and limestone sea stacks
      Two Ways Home     a lighthouse on the cliff
      Sinking Feeling   a croc in the drowned cave
      Red Centre        a road train and a camel string
      Termite Towers    the big red rock on the horizon
      Billabong         a paddle steamer
      The Last Burrow   the homestead again, windpump, tank
    The rest:
      out bush   windpumps, water tanks, tin sheds, a rotary clothes line,
                 drum letterboxes, fences, a BILBY CROSSING sign, grass
                 trees, wattle, banksia, boabs, termite mounds, roos, emus,
                 galahs overhead
      at night   the moon, the Southern Cross, a ruined homestead chimney,
                 campfires with the billy on, swag tents
      the water  herons in the reeds
      below      goldfields mine timbers, ore carts on rails, miners' lamps,
                 picks and shovels, glints of gold, sleeping bats
    They're kept as a small packed library in bank B and stamped onto
    open air only, so they never change where a bilby can walk.
  * The split-screen idea: the VZ's only interrupt comes at the end of the
    picture, so a text panel at the bottom would mean cycle-counting through
    almost the whole frame, and one at the top costs about half of every
    frame waiting, on the green text background. A mode(1) panel with its
    own small font was clearer and costs nothing.

THE RULES (as Lemmings; distances in VZ pixels, 2 dots wide x 3 lines tall)
  walkers step up 2 rows and down 3 before they fall; falls of 24+ rows are
  fatal (floaters are safe); the edges of the map turn walkers round and
  can't be climbed; climbers go up any wall and over the top;
  blockers turn everyone round; builders lay 12 sticks (a step up every 2),
  and warn on the last 3; bashers tunnel sideways, miners diagonally down,
  diggers straight down - none of them through steel; bombers count down
  5 seconds, say "oh no" and blow a hole; water drowns; the release rate
  can't go below the level's own; nuke ends the level. The game runs at
  12.5 ticks a second (the Spectrum managed about 9). Animation frames per
  action are the Spectrum's counts: walk 8, fall 4, float 8, climb 8,
  climb-over 8, block 8, build 16, shrug 8, bash 32, mine 24, dig 16,
  splat 16, drown 16, exit 8, oh-no 16.

  Every level has been played through to a win in the emulator by a script
  (tools/test.py runs all 24).

KNOWN LIMITS / THINGS TO CHECK ON REAL HARDWARE
  * Tested only in the emulator model of the VZ300 + BennVenn.
  * With 30-40 bilbies all on screen the game slows by about a fifth.
  * Not yet tried on a real BennVenn with bank B (mapping 3): if your
    board's banks differ, the start-up check will say so.
  * Progress (which levels are open) is kept until reset; no save.
  * No music during play (the speaker player needs the whole CPU): music on
    the title, briefing and results screens, sound effects in the game.

BUILDING
  ./build.sh  (needs sjasmplus and zx0). tools/gen.py makes the data
  (art.py: sprites, font, panel, textures; levels.py + levels_data.py:
  the levels and their solution scripts; lvlkit.py: the level kit and a
  Python copy of the builder, which tools/verify.py checks pixel for pixel
  against the Z80 one;
  music.py: the tunes), src/main.asm is assembled, tools/package.py makes
  BILBIES.VZ. emu/vzemu.c is the test emulator; tools/test.py plays the
  levels by script.
