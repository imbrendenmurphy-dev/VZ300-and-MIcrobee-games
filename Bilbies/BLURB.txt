BILBIES!  for the Dick Smith VZ300
==================================

I asked an AI to port Lemmings to the VZ300. It wouldn't copy Lemmings,
so I had it make an original one for the VZ instead: the same kind of
puzzle, with everything else new.

THE STORY
Out past the last fence post, where the red dirt runs to the sky, the
bilbies have lost their way home. Their burrows are scattered right
across the country - under the plains, through the gullies, down the old
goldfields shafts, past the homestead and out to the beach - and the
little marsupials have no sense of direction whatsoever. Once they start
walking, they don't stop.

That's where you come in. Give each one the right job at the right
moment - dig, build, bash, mine, climb, float, block, or (sorry, mate)
blow up - and lead as many as you can down to safety. Across 24 big
levels, from a gentle first dig to a nine-minute trek with forty
bilbies, every burrow is waiting.

WHAT'S IN IT
* 24 original levels in four ratings: MILD (out in the scrub), TRICKY
  (under the ground), TOUGH (night falls) and WILD (the big country)
* 8 skills: climber, floater, bomber, blocker, builder, basher, miner,
  digger
* Big maps - 512 x 80 pixels each, four screens across and nearly two
  down - with smooth four-way scrolling and a minimap you can click
* Up to 40 bilbies on the go at once, with full animation: 8 walk
  frames, 16 for building, 32 for bashing, 24 for mining and more
* Australiana everywhere: 50 hand-drawn scenery pictures and set pieces
  - a station homestead, a country pub with iron lace verandahs, a
  shearing shed, windpumps and water tanks, a road train and a camel
  string, a goldfields poppet head, Wave Rock, Uluru on the horizon, a
  paddle steamer, a lighthouse, a harbour beach with the flags out, and
  the Opera House and Harbour Bridge across the water - plus gum trees,
  grass trees, roos, emus, galahs, a croc and the outback dunny
* Faint sky, star, mist and cave textures behind the action, so the
  scenery never gets in the way of the bilbies
* Animated title screen with a three-voice beeper arrangement of Grieg's
  "In the Hall of the Mountain King", plus original jingles and sound
  effects
* Attract mode that plays five levels by itself, a trainer to open every
  level, a level menu with a picture of each map, and a briefing for
  every level
* Keyboard or joystick (both ports)

TECH SPECS
* Machine: Dick Smith VZ300 with the BennVenn SD loader and 128K RAM
  expansion (needs both 10K SRAM banks at $4000-$67FF)
* Display: mode(1), 128 x 64 in 4 colours - a 128 x 44 scrolling
  playfield above a status line, a 12-button skill panel and a minimap
* One file, BILBIES.VZ (23K). It loads low at $8000, copies the levels
  into the second SRAM bank, then unpacks the program in place - nothing
  runs from video RAM
* 100% Z80 assembly. The game logic runs at 12.5 ticks a second and the
  screen redraws at 25 frames a second
* Each level is built on the fly from a compact description - ground,
  rock, caves, stalactites, strata, lit edges and scenery - packed with
  ZX0 into SRAM bank B. The 512 x 80 picture is built into SRAM bank A,
  and a 1-bit-per-pixel map of solid ground sits in main RAM. A level
  builds in 1-2 seconds while the briefing is up
* Sound: everything through the VZ's 1-bit speaker; the title music
  plays three voices at once
* Played on real VZ300 hardware through every version, and every level
  has also been played through to a win by script in an emulator of the
  VZ300 + BennVenn

CONTROLS
  Q A O P / joystick   move the cursor; push an edge to scroll
  SPACE / M / FIRE     give the selected skill to a bilby
  1-8                  pick a skill        Z / X  previous / next skill
  ,  .                 release rate        H      pause
  N N                  nuke                CTRL+Q give up
  Title: FIRE play, T trainer, K music, S sound effects
