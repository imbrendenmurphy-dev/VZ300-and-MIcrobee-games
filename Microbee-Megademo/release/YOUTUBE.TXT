MICROBEE MEGADEMO 2026 - VIBED BY BPM 2026
The first megademo for the Microbee Premium Plus.

Six minutes of an Australian 8-bit doing things it was never meant to do. The Microbee has no sprites, no scroll registers, no copper and no blitter: just a Z80, a 6545 CRTC, a 1-bit speaker and a megabyte of RAM. Every effect here comes from reprogramming the CRTC in the middle of the picture, in time with the beam, scanline by scanline, while a two-voice beeper soundtrack keeps playing in the gaps.

Prepare to stress test your emulators. This demo depends on scanline-exact 6545 behaviour: start addresses re-read on every line of a character row, row counts compared all the time, and the CPU held whenever it touches video RAM while the screen is being drawn. If your emulator rolls, tears or shows a blank screen, it is the emulator.

This video was captured from a raster-accurate emulator, with the original beeper audio. The demo has been tested on a real Premium Plus with an amber monochrome monitor; the colour version shown here has only been checked in an emulator so far.

THE PARTS
Power on - the picture tube warms up: a dot, a line, a screen. Up to 222 one-line screens a frame.
Self test - a terminal checks the machine, then the picture tears apart row by row.
Title - a copper-bar logo screen with a smooth scroller.
Twister - a twisting bar, 1 pixel accurate. No pixels are drawn: the CRTC picks one of 30 slices for each scanline, 222 screens per frame.
Plasma - 1536 blocks of colour recomputed 25 times a second, inside the scanlines, between the music samples.
Kefrens bars - racing the beam: every line painted as it is drawn.
Live rotozoomer - real time, two blocks per scanline, worked out as the beam draws it. Five textures in turn (the BPM sign, honeycomb, checkers, acid smileys, tartan); each next texture is built in the background while it runs.
Tunnel, perspective floor, swirl, twist - effects no 3.375 MHz Z80 could compute in time, so every block of every frame was computed in advance into the megabyte: 324 pictures, 288K, streamed back at 25 frames a second with colour cycling.
Vector torus - a lit, dithered torus in hi-res PCG pixels: 68 pictures streamed into the hidden PCG bank, four bytes per scanline, double buffered.
Credits - a smooth vertical scroller.
Power off - the tube shrinks to a line, then a dot.

TECH SPECS
Machine: Microbee Premium Plus, 1 MB RAM, 32K PCG RAM, DS80 disk.
CPU: Z80A at 3.375 MHz. Every scanline is exactly 216 T-states, and the demo counts them all.
Display: 6545 CRTC, 64 x 16 characters of 8 x 16 PCG glyphs (512 x 256 pixels), reprogrammed up to every scanline from a line program rebuilt every frame.
Tricks: one-line frames for a start address per scanline; variable-height frames for fine vertical scrolling; extra undisplayed rows for a stable blanking edge mid-frame; video RAM writes in one burst per scanline, re-synchronised to the CRTC after the picture.
Sound: two-voice 1-bit beeper music (a pulse lead, a square bass and drums) played inside every scanline. About 6 minutes of music in 185 patterns, 5.1K.
Every part has a colour version and a monochrome version (dot patterns for mono monitors). SPACE skips to the next part.

MEMORY USED
Program, music and tables: 23.7K
Variables, line programs, scratch and stack: about 5K
Loaded from disk: 600K (graphics, screens, tiles and text 140K; tunnel 96K; floor, swirl and twist 192K; torus 128K; the rotozoomer's first texture 32K)
Built while it runs: 160K (four rotozoomer textures and the plasma's rotated colour ramps)
In use: about 790K of the 1024K
PCG RAM: 3 banks permanent (the font and two line-pattern banks), up to 5 more reloaded for each part
Disk: 628K of the DS80 disk's 784K

RUNNING IT
Boot the MEGA.DS8 disk image on a Microbee Premium Plus (real disk, SD card or floppy emulator) and type MEGA at the A prompt. Loading takes about 40 seconds. Press C for a colour monitor or M for monochrome.
